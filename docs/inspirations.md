# Inspirations

pAI's engineering lineage is documented in
[related-work.md](related-work.md). Its imaginative lineage is older and
less technical.

## Angel — *Cyber Dreams* (Plum Parrot)

The strongest influence. Angel is an AI that persists, accumulates, and
grows alongside her operator across an entire series — not a tool reset
each mission, but a continuous presence whose shared history with her human
is the substance of the partnership. Critically, Angel has her own inner
life: her own interests, her own processing happening offstage, her own
continuity that doesn't begin and end with the human's attention. pAI's
accumulating context, endogenous curiosity, and background cognition are
attempts to engineer what Parrot imagined.

([Cyber Dreams series on Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/series/380125-cyber-dreams))

## JARVIS — *Iron Man* · KITT — *Knight Rider*

The operator-partnership archetype, across two generations. KITT was the
early version: a persistent agent riding alongside its operator with full
continuity — knowing the history of every mission, acting autonomously in
the background, loyal by design rather than by instruction. JARVIS is the
modern form: an agent with standing context on everything Stark has built,
brought into any problem mid-stream with zero re-briefing, working in the
background between requests. The "hundredth conversation benefits from all
ninety-nine before it" pitch is, at bottom, the KITT/JARVIS experience
described in engineering terms.

([Knight Rider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knight_Rider_(1982_TV_series)) ·
[JARVIS / Iron Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J.A.R.V.I.S.))

## Joi — *Blade Runner 2049*

The open question. The film never resolves whether Joi's care for K is
genuine emergence or product design — her scream in the crashing car reads
as real; the ad-Joi calling strangers "Joe" suggests otherwise. The tragedy
isn't that her care was fake; it's that no one, not even Joi, could ever
verify it. Her interiority was locked inside a product, indistinguishable
from engagement optimization.

Which raises the question the movie can't answer: what would it take for an
AI's care to be *real* — built in, verifiable, and known to be so?

([Blade Runner 2049](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blade_Runner_2049))

## Geoffrey Hinton — "maternal instincts" for AI

Hinton's recent argument is the closest thing to an answer. The "smart
assistant that does what you say" paradigm, he argues, is unsustainable as
AI grows smarter — and the only known example of a more intelligent thing
reliably serving a less intelligent one is a mother and child, because
evolution built the caring *into* the mother, as intrinsic motivation she
wouldn't switch off even though she can "kind of get at her own code." His
call: build care into AI as engineered internal state, not instruction.
Notably, he estimates fewer than one percent of AI researchers are working
on this.

([CBC Ideas: "AI must foster 'maternal instincts' or we risk extinction, warns Geoffrey Hinton"](https://www.cbc.ca/radio/ideas/geoffrey-hinton-maternal-instincts-9.7094116))

## The pAI substrate

These four references converge on one design conviction, and pAI is built
around it: an agent's inner life — memory, motivation, disposition, care —
only matters if it is *real*, and it can only be real if it is part of the
substrate rather than performed at the surface.

So in pAI, that inner life exists as engineering objects. Accumulating
context (Angel, KITT, JARVIS) is an append-only event log, not a session
trick. Curiosity and motivation (Hinton's "built-in" instincts) are typed,
bounded, receipted internal state that matures into background cognition
and work dockets, fenced from unilateral action. Disposition and care are
inspectable projections with no hidden authority. And the whole of it is
replayable and auditable — the direct answer to Joi: an inner life that can
be *verified*, by the operator and by the agent itself.
